What is a Confidence Interval?

A confidence interval is the range of values that is likely to contain a population parameter with a certain level of confidence.

Recall how Sampling gradually “hones in” on our true value as we add observations:

The line “jitters” around, showing that there is some uncertainty in the estimate, but this uncertainty gets smaller & smaller as our sample gets bigger.

If we plotted our confidence interval, we’d see that, as we add more observations, our confidence interval becomes tighter.

However, __this improvement quickly shrinks__ as we add more observations, and eventually each new data point does very little to increase our confidence.

Samples vs. Populations

It’s important to note that our sample and population are 2 very different things:

  • The standard deviation of the population does not change, no matter how big our sample is. This variability is inherent to our data.
  • The population mean has no confidence interval — if we were to measure every person’s height, then the mean would be a single value.
  • The thing we become more sure of as we add data to our sample is the range within the population value should fall. (This is our confidence interval!)

Confidence Intervals in Daily Life

One practical lesson of confidence intervals that you can take away is:

Do small tests, and do more of them.

As we’ve discussed, sampling gives us a hugely valuable way to estimate values with only a few data points. We also saw how surprisingly quickly the value of each additional observation drops off.
